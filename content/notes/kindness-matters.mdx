The world does not need another reason for people to feel worse about themselves.

There are already enough.

Every opinion becomes an identity. Every disagreement becomes a reason to hate someone. People assume the worst about anyone outside their group and spend more time speaking at each other than understanding each other.

At the same time, people are becoming more secluded, more lonely, and more emotionally exhausted. We have more ways to contact each other than any generation in history and still manage to make a lot of people feel invisible.

Kindness has never felt more crucial than it does right now.

**The Scale of What People Are Carrying**

The mental-health numbers do not rise in a perfectly straight line every year. Saying that would be inaccurate. The longer-term direction and current scale are still impossible to ignore.

The [CDC reports](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-youth/mental-health/mental-health-numbers.html) that four in ten U.S. high school students experienced persistent sadness or hopelessness in 2023, and two in ten seriously considered attempting suicide. The [overall U.S. suicide rate](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db509.htm) increased 30 percent from 2002 to 2018, declined for two years, and returned to 14.2 deaths per 100,000 people in 2022. The [World Health Organization reported](https://www.who.int/news/item/02-03-2022-covid-19-pandemic-triggers-25-increase-in-prevalence-of-anxiety-and-depression-worldwide) that the first year of the COVID-19 pandemic produced a 25 percent global increase in the prevalence of anxiety and depression.

The [U.S. Surgeon General's social-connection advisory](https://www.hhs.gov/surgeongeneral/reports-and-publications/connection/index.html) describes loneliness and social isolation as serious threats to health and well-being. Loneliness is not simply an uncomfortable mood. Connection affects stress, mental health, physical health, and whether someone feels seen.

You cannot see most of that by looking at someone.

The person standing beside you may be carrying grief, rejection, family problems, insecurity, anxiety, or a private fight to get through the day. They may look completely normal while feeling invisible.

That is one reason a small act can matter far beyond what it costs.

**The Science Is Not Complicated**

Humans are built for connection.

Prosocial behavior includes helping, encouraging, giving, and performing acts of kindness. A [2023 systematic review and meta-analysis in JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2812720) found positive health outcomes associated with prosocial interventions. A separate [meta-analysis of 201 studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32881540/) found a small but meaningful positive relationship between prosocial behavior and well-being, especially psychological and meaning-centered well-being.

That does not mean one compliment cures depression. It means kindness participates in the systems that help people feel connected, useful, valued, and human.

When you help someone feel seen, you create connection. When you act like your presence can improve the environment around you, you strengthen your own sense of purpose. When you look for good things to say, your attention begins finding good things to notice.

The benefit moves in both directions.

**Three Compliments a Day**

For many months now, I have tried to compliment three people every day.

I do not prepare the lines or force something that is not true. I look for something genuine and say it when I notice it.

You look good today.

I like your shoes.

I respect your confidence.

I am praying for you.

You handled that really well.

The words usually take two seconds.

The reaction can change my entire mood. Someone smiles immediately or looks surprised, like they were not expecting anyone to notice them at all.

Those smiles genuinely make my day. The habit has never failed to put me in a better mood because I get to watch a small amount of care become visible on another person's face.

Nothing was lost. Both people leave feeling better.

**Why We Hold Back**

I think a lot of kindness gets stopped by unnecessary hesitation.

People worry a compliment will sound awkward. They notice something good and keep it inside because saying it feels slightly uncomfortable.

The possible awkwardness lasts a second. The encouragement can stay with someone all day.

I have received compliments I still remember years later. The person who gave one probably forgot before the day ended. A sentence that cost almost nothing became part of how I saw myself when I needed it.

We rarely know when we are creating that kind of moment for someone else.

**Kindness Is Not Agreement**

Being kind does not require abandoning your beliefs or pretending every action is acceptable.

You can disagree and remain respectful. You can enforce a boundary without trying to destroy a person. You can tell the truth without enjoying the pain it causes. Kindness is not weakness. It is control over the part of you that wants to make another person feel small just because you can.

That control is becoming rare.

Online, cruelty is rewarded with attention. People become abstractions on a screen instead of human beings with families, fears, histories, and lives that continue after the comment ends.

Kindness brings the person back into view.

**Try It Daily**

If something takes two seconds and makes another human feel better about themselves 99.99 percent of the time, why would you not do it?

Start with one person. Say the good thing you were already thinking. Thank someone specifically. Tell a friend what you respect about them. Encourage the person who seems quiet. Compliment the stranger's shoes. Tell someone you are praying for them, and mean it.

Then do it again tomorrow.

Kindness will not solve every mental-health crisis or replace the help someone may need. It can still change the direction of a moment. Enough moments become the environment people live inside.

You have more influence over that environment than you think.

Be the reason someone feels seen. Be the person who makes a room lighter without needing credit for it. Make kindness part of your daily life until looking for the good in people becomes automatic.

It can change the lives around you.

It will change yours too.
